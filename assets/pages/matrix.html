The Matrix is the term of the worldwide computer network of the Sixth World. It was created after the First Matrix Crash of 2029 and rebuilt as the Wireless Matrix after the Crash 2.0 of 2064. The Matrix consists of countless data streams and icons and is accessed either indirectly via Augmented Reality, overlaying and enhancing elements of the real world, or through Virtual Reality, where a user can project their minds directly into a physical manifestation of the global net.

Most people use the Matrix extensively in their daily lives; shopping, communicating, even attending parties in Virtual Reality. Most people interface with the net through their commlinks; small, handheld devices that communicate the will of their users with the underlying foundation. Special glasses, gloves and earbuds are cheap and let people interact with digital objects in real space, but are steadily replaced by trode networks and almost non-invasive datajacks; these devices directly read the brainwaves of their users, and can directly interface the users brain to the Matrix. Most people do not even touch their devices these days, only interacting with the Matrix through pop-up displays, voice interfaces, and thoughts.

Since everyone does everything on the net nowadays, deckers are widely feared for their ability to manipulate the data streams to their advantage. Despite the name, deckers do not necessarily need to employ cyberdecks; a run-off-the-mill commlink often does the job well enough, and is not nearly as expensive as a professional deck.

Terms and Basics

The following encompasses a few terms that are important to know when interacting with the Matrix. While the detailed systems about how things work might be more interesting for runners interacting with the Matrix on an everyday basis, such as journalists, riggers, and of course deckers, it doesn't hurt a magician to know how their fancy pocket computer works or why it hurts when they get slammed by a digital battleaxe.

Personas

A persona is the embodiment of a person, inside of the Matrix. Even when a person does not have any devices on themselves, they still weirdly enough have a persona (probably due to nanites in the air or something). The persona provides the main access of a person to the Matrix, while their commlink (or any similar device) provides an interface to direct that persona.

Usually, the location of a persona overlaps with the physical location of a person in realspace. Therefore, when attempting to interact with another persona in realspace, two people need to be able to see each other. Deckers trying to hack another persona or any device in realspace (while in AR) therefore must have a line of sight to their target, which is limited to Long range.

Damaging and Repairing a Persona

Each persona has a System Strain threshold. System Strain can can be affliced by malicious code, unfortunate timings, or general strain on the local network. If the System Strain threshold is reached or exceeded, the persona is destroyed, and the personas’ owner loses access to the Matrix, being considered offline. Additionally, the device that the personas' owner used to interface with the Matrix is damaged by one step.

Repairing a damaged persona takes time and little bit of Mechanical knowledge. When you attempt to repair a Persona, make a Mechanics skill check, with a difficulty according to the table below:

Being Offline

Due to the general omnipresence of the Matrix, most modern devices cannot really handle any offline capacity. If a device is able to function correctly without a Matrix connection is up to the Gamemasters discretion, but almost always includes out-of-the-box firearms, vehicles, drones, most gear and almost certainly bodytech. If such a device loses it's access to the net, one of two things happen. Either if becomes straight-up unusable, like a commlink, or it becomes rather risky to use due to malfunctioning software; guns misfire, medkits violently hack into people, and cars become uncontrollable without a physical steering wheel (yuck). If this happens, a person attempting to use such a device upgrades the difficulty of all checks made with the offline device twice.

AR and VR

Since everyone nowadays is equipped with technology that allows them to interact with the Matrix without touching any physical device, everyone is able to enter Virtual Reality at any time they want. When someone enters VR, they leave their unconscious bodies behind and become one with their personas, allowing someone in VR to move their virtual bodies around independently from their physical bodies, as long as they are aware of their digital surroundings. Switching interaction modes always takes a maneuver.

Advantages of VR

Risks of VR

Using VR comes with a few risks. The first and most immediate risk is that a decker entering VR leave their physical bodies behind, leaving them unprotected and vulnerable. This can range from a minor inconvenience when they do so from the security of your own home, to borderline suicidal if you do this while in an active firefight. In practice, this means that all attacks against your physical body are upgraded twice. Additionally, all effects that effect an area, such as the Blast quality, automatically hit you once, even if the a required to trigger the effect are not generated. Its best to have a strong back or strong walls to rely to.

The second risk of using VR is Biofeedback. If a persona of a person in VR suffers system strain from any source, they suffer an equivalent amount of wounds as well. If a persona is destroyed by the resulting System Strain, the decker suffers a Jackout.

Which leads to the third risk: Jackouts. While most devices can be interacted with from afar, some targets, specifically hosts, require the decker to physically wire their brains to a device connected to a to the target. When this connection is involuntarily cut for whatever reason, the decker immediately and violently snaps back to the real world, suffers eight system strain and wounds, which cannot be reduced by their firewall or soak, and receives a Critical Injury, adding 50 to the result of the roll. If this doesn't incapacitate them, they are immobilized and staggered until the end of the encounter (or at least the next two minutes).

(Ab)using the Matrix

People interact with the Matrix through their personas, which they control using a device. While a true decker prefers to use a cyberdeck, the unfortunate truth (or fortunate, whoever it is who you are asking) is, that a commlink is all it takes to wreak havoc in the Matrix; cyberdecks are just better at that.

Generally speaking, interacting with the Matrix uses one of two skills: Computers or Hacking. Computers is used to use the Matrix in a safe and legal way: Writing to a blog, searching for information online, writing software and similar. Hacking on the other end is used to break stuff. Using it counts as the digital equivalent of shooting a gun, and encompasses various methods of breaking and subverting established Matrix security protocols. It's also used by the Good Guys With A Deck to protect systems against hostile deckers.

Matrix Actions

Difficulty of Matrix Actions

All matrix checks fall into one of two categories: Matrix combat checks, and Matrix action checks. Matrix combat checks are made when you want to apply brute force against your target. They are come out fast, and essentially throw your malicious code at your adversaries, and are usually best used when drek has already hit the wall. Like the name implies, they are treated as combat checks with a range of Long, and their difficulty is usually equal to normal combat checks, with a targets Firewall rating acting as both Soak and Defense against suffered system strain and hits.

Matrix actions encompass all other types of checks. They are usually much more stealthy that Matrix combat checks, but usually require you or your persona to be within Short range of your target. When you make a Matrix action against a target - usually because you want to access a device, host or files from within said device or host without proper authorization - you make an Easy (k) Hacking or Computers skill check, and increase the difficulty by one for each point of Firewall the target has (you do not add j per point of Firewall like you would with Matrix combat checks).

The Gamemaster can increase or decrease the difficulty of the check, or add additional j or j based on environmental details; the total of these effects are too numerous to list here, but you and your Gamemaster can use the table "Difficulty of Matrix skill checks" to get a rough overview of example difficulties and modifiers.

List of Matrix Maneuvers

Like normal Maneuvers in meatspace, these maneuvers typically do not require dice rolls to use.

Issue Command

You issue a simple command to a device you own. At the Gamemasters discretion, more complex commands - i.e. those that require a skill check - may require an Operating skill check.

Mark Target

You place a mark on your target, creating a backdoor in your targets' security that makes further Hacking attempts easier. Until the end of the round, your next Hacking combat check adds j. You can use this maneuver multiple times per round, adding j each time.

Slot / Unslot / Change Cyberprogram

You slot, unslot, or change a cyberprogram from or to your commlink, cyberdeck or RCC.

Switch Interface Mode

You leave your body behind to fully dive into Virtual Reality, or you return to your body. Your character switches from AR to VR or vice versa.

Zoom Into Target

While in VR, as a single maneuver, regardless of range, you can move into Short range to a target within Long range of your physical location.

Access System

Roll: Hacking action check

System: You attempt to gain access to a system that is actively protected against unauthorized access, like a host or another persons device. Make a Hacking skill check against a target within Short range, where the difficulty is set by the Gamemaster based on the system in question (use the Table "Difficulty of Matrix skill checks" as a rough guideline). On a success, until the end of the scene or encounter, you have access to the system in question until you are actively removed by a Sysop (usually through cybercombat) or ICE.

Edit File

Roll: Computers action check

System: You attempt to read or write a file that you have access to. If it is a file you own, you do not have to make a check, otherwise the difficulty of the check is set by the Gamemaster, based on the system the file is on or the overall security around the file (use the Table "Difficulty of Matrix skill checks" as a rough guideline). You can also use the Edit File matrix action to use a feature of the device, such as viewing a live camera feed.

Data Spike

Roll: Hacking combat check

System: You attack a target persona, icon or device within Long range with malicious code. Make a Hacking combat check against the target. On a success, the target suffers an amount of System Strain equal to your character's Intellect rating, plus any uncancelled s. If the target does not have a System Strain monitor, it instead gets damaged by one step, plus an additional step for every uncancelled ss, which can potentially break a device in one go.
If a Data Spike targets a vehicle, drone, or causes Biofeedback, t generated on the check can be used to inflict a Critical Injury on the target.

Take Over

Roll: Hacking combat check

System: You temporarily hijack a device. Make a Hacking combat check. On a success, you may use the device as if you were it's owner until the beginning of your next turn, plus an additional turn for each uncancelled ss. If the target device is a vehicle or drone, it acts immediately after you end your turn. As part of the Take Over matrix action, you can issue a simple command as if you were using the Issue Command matrix maneuver.

Jam

Roll: Hacking combat check

System: You overload a target persona with overwhelming background noise, making attempts at communication harder. Make a Hacking combat check against the target. On a success, until the beginning of your next turn plus one additional turn for each uncancelled ss, the target cannot send or receive any messages through the Matrix, unless the succeed a Hard (kkk) Computers check first. aa generated on the check may be spent to target an additional device within range.

Hide

Roll: Opposed Hacking vs. Computers

System: You try to hide icons or their persona from Matrix perception. Make an opposed Hacking skill check against an adversaries Computers skill. On a success, all adversaries lose track of your persona and your icons, and must take an Opposed Seek matrix action to find you again.

Seek

Roll: Computers action or Opposed Computers vs. Hacking

System: You attempt to search for hidden icons and personas. Make a Computers check. On a success, you find and trace a single icon or persona you were looking for, or the most dangerous or interesting icon or persona in the designated area. aa generated on the check may be spent to find an additional icon or persona within range. If the targeted icon or persona is actively hiding from you, the check becomes an opposed skill check against the targets Hacking skill.
